## Why this paper is appropriate for Analytical Chemistry

The paper is about chemical measurement science rather than about any one
material. It makes three contributions that bear directly on how Raman
measurements are turned into numbers.

First, it removes a step that silently biases what gets analysed. Raman
hyperspectral maps are routinely archived in an undocumented vendor container
and reach analysis only through manual text export, so the analysable corpus is
the subset somebody remembered to export. We reverse engineer the Horiba
LabSpec 6 container and verify the decode bit identically against the
instrument's own export. On the corpus studied here that takes the number of
usable maps from one to twenty-three, which is the difference between a
single-map case study and a cross-sample one.

Second, it treats the preprocessing chain as part of the measurement rather
than as a detail. Every metric edge in the cross-sample graph carries the
protocol under which it was computed, and we show quantitatively why that
matters: across eight maps and five defensible baseline families, the raw
peak-height D/G ratio moves by a median of 81% of a map's own value and its
traces cross, so the protocol can reorder two samples, while the bounded
disorder index moves by 4.9%.

Third, it introduces a compact differentiable representation of a hyperspectral
map, a field of anisotropic ellipsoids in two spatial axes and wavenumber,
fitted on closed-form gradients in NumPy on a CPU. The representation is
evaluated against each map's own measurement noise rather than against PSNR
alone, and we show that PSNR across maps mostly reports how noisily each map
was measured (Pearson r = 0.83 against map signal-to-noise) rather than how
well it was represented.

The paper also reports, in a dedicated section, four defects that produced
plausible numbers rather than errors, including a particle mask that selected
the substrate on some maps and a similarity threshold that measured itself. We
believe measurement-science readers will meet the same traps, and that
reporting them is more useful than reporting only the results that survived.

## Data and code availability

The software, the pipeline, the anonymity gate and the manuscript's asset
generator are released together at
<https://github.com/siddhartha18pahari/RAMANSP>; every number in the paper is
regenerated by running them. A companion site at
<https://ramanspjs.vercel.app> carries the same material in browsable form,
including the cross-sample knowledge graph as an interactive view rather than
a static figure, every figure at full resolution, and the manuscript. The corpus is distributed in anonymised form only, and the
identity map is withheld. Raw vendor binaries are not redistributable and are
not included; the reader that decodes them is.
